## Headline

The big−small triangle direction is NOT a geometric size axis under spatially-uniform SA
injection. Applied to inverted circle photos it is a clean, monotone **saturation / ink-mass
axis**: −α fades the circle toward white, +α oversaturates it (hue drifts toward vivid
green/blue at +16); position and radius are untouched at every α. The same axis shows up in
free generation (+α = crisp, saturated, 3D-shadowed shapes on clean white; −α = washed-out blur).

## Free generation (5 shape prompts, guidance 5, α ±1..±3)

Non-white-area ratios are confounded by background cleanliness; visually (contact sheets):
+α sharpens and saturates (more, crisper shapes with shadows), −α washes out. No monotone
object-size change. prompt_01 at −2 collapses to a single square on gray.

## DDIM vs MW

Indistinguishable end-to-end: direction cosine(DDIM, MW) ≈ 0.9996–0.9999, norms match to 1%.
Flat-color shapes on white are trivial to invert, so the inversion LoRA has nothing to fix —
consistent with the style finding that MW only matters on hard inversions.

## Interpretation

Pooled [step, channel] SA features of big vs small shapes differ mainly in how much colored
content the image carries, so the fitted direction encodes "amount of ink / saturation".
Injecting one channel vector uniformly over all spatial tokens cannot move geometry, and on an
inverted trajectory at guidance 1 the anchor keeps layout fixed — only photometry moves.

## Next steps

- Size as geometry likely needs token-resolved (spatial) steering or a direction fit on
  spatially-aligned features, not pooled channels.
- The per-example activations in `extract/features.pt` support checking this: per-pair
  big−small deltas vs the pooled mean direction (is the pooled direction just the saturation
  component of a richer per-pair signal?).
- If the goal is a true size edit, a confound-free dataset variant: same ink mass, different
  radius (e.g. ring/outline shapes), to decorrelate size from colored-pixel count.
